# Loci

Turn a photo of your own room into a memory palace for a list you need to learn, then prove it worked by saying the list back.

> The learner's standing instruction: "No questions to me. If a skill asks me something, answer for me — simple." The interview answers this scope is built from were written by the agent from the learner's planning notes. Each one is listed under **Interview Record** and marked *(answered by agent per learner's standing instruction)*.

## The Unique Kernel
Your own room becomes the memory palace. From one photo, AI finds real objects (the door, the lamp, the shelf), and the app lays a walking route through them and ties each list item to one object with a short, strange scene. Then a recall test proves it worked: walk the route in your head, say or type each item, and its pin turns green.
Other memory apps give you flashcards or an imaginary palace. Here every stop is something you see every day.

## Who It's For
Me first, and students like me who have to learn ordered lists for exams: the 12 cranial nerves, the first 12 elements, the steps of a procedure.
Today we use flashcards, re-reading, and first-letter sentences ("Oh Oh Oh To Touch And Feel…"), which hold the first letters but not the words. The memory palace works, but building one by hand takes a long time, so most people never try it.

## The Core Loop
1. Pick a photo of your room and paste the list.
2. Numbered pins appear on real objects, joined by a dotted route that starts at the left of the photo and ends at the right. Each stop has a short, vivid scene.
3. **Learn:** step through the stops once, reading each scene while looking at that object.
4. **Recall:** the photo goes dark and only the route is left. At each stop, say or type the item. Right turns the pin green, wrong turns it red.
5. Retry only the red ones until every pin is green.

You come back to walk the same palace again before the exam, or to build a new one for the next list.

## Inspiration & Identity
- The method of loci, also called the memory palace: place things along a route through a place you know well, then walk the route to recall them. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_loci
- Feel: a quiet late-night study session. Calm and focused while learning; one small, bright moment each time a pin turns green.
- Not a game with points and confetti. Not a flashcard deck.

## What "Working" Looks Like
Open Loci on a phone. Pick a photo of my room and paste 12 items I have never studied. Within half a minute, pins sit on the door, the lamp, the shelf, the fan, joined by a dotted route. I tap through the 12 scenes once. Then the photo goes dark, I close my eyes and say the list out loud, and the pins turn green one after another. The result says how many I got right on the first try, how long I spent learning, and how long recalling.

The "oh, that's cool" beat: saying the list with my eyes closed while the route lights up green.

Anyone can try it in seconds with an example room and an example list, without a photo.

## The POC Boundary
- One photo makes one palace for one list: up to 12 short items, 4 words or fewer each.
- AI finds the objects and writes the scenes. Code orders the route and checks every answer.
- Learn mode: the route, the pins, one scene per stop.
- Recall mode: speak where the browser supports it; typing always works. Green and red pins, a score, retry the misses.
- Example rooms and example lists, so anyone can try it without a photo.
- Palaces are saved on the device.
- Plain messages when something fails: too few objects found, AI unreachable, no microphone.

## What the Spike Showed
A quick test before approving this scope: three AI-generated room photos (a student's room, a studio flat, a kitchen) and the 12 cranial nerves.
- Each photo gave 13–15 usable objects for 12 items, all real and clearly placed.
- A route that starts at the far left and ends at the far right, taking the shortest way between, reads as one walk. Sorting purely left to right zigzagged.
- Scenes were vivid and used sound-alikes for hard words ("trochlear" → a truck that's learning to drive).
- Still open: a real phone photo of a real room, and how well the browser hears hard words when spoken.

## Later

- A drawn picture for each scene.
- Share a palace with a study friend.

## Explicitly Cut
- **Accounts and cloud sync.** A room photo is private. It stays on the device.
- **Claims about long-term memory.** A proof of concept can only show recall in one sitting.
- **Sentence-length items** (speech lines, definitions). Checking a whole sentence, spoken or typed, can't be fair or reliable. Short items only.
- **AI judging the answers.** Code decides green or red, so the score can be trusted.
